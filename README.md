# Could AI Have Designed a Better Bottle Cap?

**What attached caps reveal about Europe’s talent for regulation—and its struggle to build new technology.**

This repository supports two connected outputs:

1. A sharp, evidence-based opinion essay about attached bottle caps, European regulation, and AI competitiveness.
2. A small reproducible experiment on how different problem framings change the solutions generated by AI systems.

## Core thesis

Europe is exceptionally capable of coordinating and enforcing specific product changes, but less effective at creating the capital, compute, procurement, experimentation, and risk-taking conditions required for frontier technology.

The bottle cap is a concrete case through which to examine the difference between two institutional activities:

- **Regulation scales a known intervention.**
- **Innovation searches among uncertain alternatives.**

The argument is not that attached caps are pointless or that AI would certainly have designed something better. The requirement addresses a real litter and recycling problem. The project asks whether a more outcome-oriented process could have explored a broader solution space—and what that reveals about European and enterprise approaches to AI.

## Proposed experiment

Compare AI responses under several framings:

1. **Blind outcome brief:** solve the environmental leakage problem without knowing the object is a bottle cap.
2. **Revealed outcome brief:** solve the same problem with the bottle-cap context visible.
3. **Prescriptive brief:** optimize a design that must keep the cap attached.
4. **Adversarial evaluation:** have a separate model challenge assumptions and propose discriminating tests.
5. **Optional human baseline:** compare idea categories and blind spots with a small mixed-background group.

The main result should be modest and measurable: whether outcome framing broadens the searched solution space, and whether evaluation requirements improve testability and uncertainty discipline.

## Editorial guardrails

The article should:

- acknowledge the environmental rationale before criticizing institutional habits;
- distinguish policy outcomes from prescribed mechanisms;
- treat AI concepts as hypotheses, not validated engineering solutions;
- avoid claiming that one small experiment proves anything general about Europe;
- separate experiment findings from the essay’s broader political interpretation.

## Practical next steps

1. Finalize the prompts and freeze protocol version 1.
2. Select three models from at least two model families.
3. Run five repetitions per condition and save all raw outputs.
4. Pilot the rubric with two raters and revise ambiguous definitions.
5. Analyze category breadth, within-response diversity, testability, feasibility, and unsupported confidence.
6. Research the legal history, impact assessment, post-implementation evidence, and alternative interventions.
7. Draft the essay after results are available, whether they support or weaken the initial thesis.
